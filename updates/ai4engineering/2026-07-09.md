# AI for Engineering Weekly Update — July 9, 2026

## Highlights

- **Figure hits one robot per hour at BotQ**: Figure AI scaled Figure 03 production to one humanoid per hour — a 24x throughput increase in under 120 days — while retiring its F.02 fleet after nearly a year at BMW Spartanburg, where the robots supported production of 30,000+ BMW X3s.
- **Humanoid mega-rounds continue**: Apptronik closed a $935M round at a $5.5B+ valuation for manufacturing and logistics humanoids; Shenzhen's AI2 Robotics raised ~$735M at nearly $3B.
- **A humanoid maker goes public**: A humanoid robotics company announced its IPO this week (TechCrunch, July 5), with its CEO notably tempering expectations for home robots — the industry's center of gravity remains industrial.

## Detailed Updates

### Figure 03 production ramp and BMW milestone
Figure AI retired its F.02 robots after deploying them at BMW's Spartanburg plant for nearly a year, where they contributed to production of over 30,000 BMW X3 vehicles and loaded more than 90,000 sheet metal parts. The successor Figure 03 is ramping with expanding paid deployments, including sequencing tasks at Spartanburg, and BotQ factory output has reached one robot per hour. (humanoid.press, kraneshares.com)

### Boston Dynamics' electric Atlas is fully committed
Boston Dynamics retired the iconic hydraulic Atlas and unveiled its all-electric successor designed for commercial deployment, with movement range exceeding human capability. Atlas 2026 production is fully committed, with initial fleets deploying to Hyundai's RMAC and Google DeepMind. (humanoid.press)

### Funding: Apptronik $935M, AI2 Robotics $735M
Austin-based Apptronik closed a $935M round valuing it above $5.5B for manufacturing and logistics humanoids. Shenzhen's AI2 Robotics, maker of wheeled humanoids, raised roughly $735M at a near-$3B valuation. China is pushing national deployment goals of 10k+ humanoids by end-2026, with production forecasts exceeding 100k units — affordable Chinese volume contrasting with high-value industrial focus from Figure, Boston Dynamics, and Tesla. (techcrunch.com, kraneshares.com)

### Berkeley Lab's digital twin compresses discovery from months to minutes
Berkeley Lab's Digital Twin for Chemical Science (DTCS) creates a digital replica of ambient-pressure X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy, letting researchers observe reactions, adjust parameters, and validate hypotheses in real time during a single experiment — for example analyzing compounds forming on an operating battery's surface. The platform could compress discovery timelines from months to minutes. (newscenter.lbl.gov)

### Physics-informed AI invents rare-earth-free magnets
Ames Laboratory developed DuctGPT, a physics-trained model used to discover rare-earth-free permanent magnets. Because it understands underlying physics, it can propose new materials while accounting for production costs and component sourcing — an example of the shift from brute-force screening to physics-informed generative discovery. (technology.org)

### SK hynix builds toward autonomous fabs with NVIDIA
SK hynix will use NVIDIA tools to accelerate semiconductor simulation and build digital twins for autonomous fab operations, part of a broader wave of AI-driven simulation in high-value manufacturing highlighted around NVIDIA's National Robotics Week research roundup. (crescendo.ai, blogs.nvidia.com)
