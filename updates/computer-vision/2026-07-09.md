# Computer Vision Weekly Update — July 9, 2026

## Highlights

- **Meta launches Muse Image, previews Muse Video**: Meta's most advanced image model yet operates as an agent — invoking search and coding tools to improve accuracy and self-refining its generations — and ships with Content Seal, an invisible watermark that survives cropping, compression, and screenshots.
- **Seedance 2.5 sets the pace in video generation**: ByteDance's model offers native 30-second 4K output, up to 50 reference images, and native audio, currently the most talked-about video model in a field that includes Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0 Turbo, and Runway Gen-4.5.
- **4D scene reconstruction research accelerates for autonomous driving**: New papers including DynamicVGGT, DriveTok, and VGGDrive push dynamic point maps, unified scene tokenization, and geometric grounding for vision-language driving models.

## Detailed Updates

### Muse Image: agentic image generation with provenance built in
Meta launched Muse Image, which follows instructions faithfully, edits with precision, composes from multiple references, and draws on Instagram for social context. Distinctively, it works as an agent that calls search and coding tools to improve accuracy and iteratively refines its own outputs. Every image carries Content Seal, a hidden provenance signal robust to cropping, compression, resizing, and screenshotting. Meta is also previewing Muse Video, competitive on prompt adherence, visual fidelity, and temporal consistency. (ai.meta.com, about.fb.com)

### Video generation: native audio and real camera control go mainstream
The first half of 2026 saw video models cross into production-grade territory. Seedance 2.5 leads with native 30s 4K, 50 reference images, and native audio; Kling 3.0 supports native 4K, 60fps, 15-second clips, and multilingual lip-sync. Veo 3.1, Runway Gen-4.5, and Gemini Omni Flash round out the leading pack. Analysts note the distance between "AI-generated" and "agency-grade" closed faster than most creative teams could adapt. (hedra.com, buildmvpfast.com)

### Driving-scene reconstruction: from static maps to 4D
Fresh arXiv work targets dynamic scene understanding for autonomous vehicles: DynamicVGGT learns dynamic point maps for 4D reconstruction, FRUC does feedforward reconstruction from uncalibrated collaborative driving views, DriveTok tokenizes 3D driving scenes for unified multi-view reconstruction and understanding, and VGGDrive adds cross-view geometric grounding to vision-language models. The DriveX and CVPR WAD workshops are centering 3D/4D Gaussian splatting and cooperative perception as foundation-model priorities. (arxiv.org, cvpr2026.wad.vision)

### Open models drive vision research at ICML 2026
NVIDIA's ICML 2026 roundup highlights how open models are increasingly driving vision and multimodal research, with Google also presenting a large slate. Open-weight releases are narrowing the gap with closed labs in perception tasks, echoing the broader open-model momentum seen in 3D generation (Microsoft's TRELLIS.2). (blogs.nvidia.com, research.google)

### CVPR VGBE 2026 launches image-to-video consistency benchmark
A new Codabench competition at CVPR's VGBE workshop targets image-to-video consistent generation — a sign the field is formalizing evaluation of temporal consistency, one of the last major quality gaps in generative video. (codabench.org)
