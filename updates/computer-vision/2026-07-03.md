# Computer Vision Weekly Update — July 3, 2026

## Highlights

- **ByteDance releases Seedance 2.5**: The new video model adds local editing and native 30-second generation, underscoring that editing — local scene repair, clip extension, object replacement — has become the real battleground in video generation, not just raw quality.
- **Google's image-model double release**: Gemini 3.1 Flash Image and Gemini 3 Pro Image shipped June 30 through AI Studio and the Gemini API, alongside DeepMind's Nano Banana 2 Lite, its fastest and most cost-efficient image generator.
- **CVPR 2026 shatters records**: The conference drew 16,092 paper submissions, up 24% year-over-year, with 4,089 accepted presentations — a measure of how much the field keeps accelerating.

## Detailed Updates

### Video generation's editing era
ByteDance's Seedance 2.5 launch (local editing, native 30s clips) marks a shift: as base generation quality converges, differentiation is moving to controllability and editing workflows. It joins a 2026 field that includes Kling 3.0 (native 4K, multilingual audio, top of the Artificial Analysis text-to-video leaderboard), Lightricks' open-source LTX-2.3 (native 4K with open weights), and Google's budget Veo 3.1 Lite. ([WaveSpeed](https://wavespeed.ai/blog/posts/ai-video-generation-news-2026/))

### Image generation: 2K–4K becomes table stakes
The current leaderboard has OpenAI's GPT Image 2 on top of the Artificial Analysis Image Arena (Elo 1339, the largest first-to-second gap recorded), with Google's Nano Banana Pro, Black Forest Labs' FLUX.2, Midjourney V8.1, and Stable Diffusion 3.5 each holding market slices. Midjourney V8.1 now renders 2048×2048 by default; Nano Banana Pro pushes 4096×4096. Google's new Gemini 3.1 Flash Image and Gemini 3 Pro Image add aggressive API pricing tiers. ([AI Flash Report](https://aiflashreport.com/ai-image-generators.html), [llm-stats](https://llm-stats.com/llm-updates))

### CVPR 2026 wraps with record numbers
CVPR 2026 closed with 16,092 submissions (+24% YoY) and 4,089 accepted papers. Dynamic-scene reconstruction from ordinary moving-camera video was among the themes drawing attention, feeding digital twins and robotics applications. ([CVPR](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/News/Closing))

### Autonomous driving research: geometry meets VLMs
Recent arXiv work shows vision-language and geometry converging for driving: VGGDrive adds cross-view geometric grounding to VLMs; DriveTok tokenizes 3D driving scenes for unified multi-view reconstruction and understanding; and DVGT-2 presents a vision-geometry-action model for driving at scale. ([VGGDrive](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.20794), [DriveTok](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.19219), [DVGT-2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.00813))

### Optical computing for machine vision
Nature coverage highlights optical computing prototypes achieving accurate real-time perception across diverse tasks — a candidate path to rapid, low-energy, on-device vision intelligence that sidesteps conventional GPU inference. ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01891-0))

### Medical imaging: brain-metastases AI cleared
On June 29 the FDA cleared MRI-based AI software for assessment of brain metastases. The agency has now authorized over 1,450 AI-enabled medical devices, with radiology accounting for roughly 76% of clearances — though reimbursement continues to lag approvals. ([Diagnostic Imaging](https://www.diagnosticimaging.com/view/fda-clears-mri-based-ai-software-assessment-brain-metastases), [Radiology Business](https://radiologybusiness.com/topics/artificial-intelligence/radiology-dominates-fda-cleared-ai-reimbursement-lags-far-behind))

### MIT: efficient 3D mapping
MIT researchers paired an efficient algorithm with dedicated hardware to rapidly generate 3D maps for navigation using minimal memory and power (June 25) — relevant to embedded vision on drones and robots. ([MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/topic/machine-learning))
