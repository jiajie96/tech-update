# Computer Vision Weekly Update — July 4, 2026

## Highlights

- **Video generation leaderboard reshuffles**: ByteDance's Seedance 2.0 now tops the Artificial Analysis with-audio leaderboard, with Alibaba's HappyHorse-1.0 leading without-audio — displacing incumbents Sora 2 and Veo 3.1 in a fast-moving four-way race.
- **Kling 3.0 pushes native 4K**: Kuaishou's latest model delivers native 4K, 60fps, 15-second clips with multilingual lip-sync, as Alibaba readies Wan 3.0 (60B parameters, 30-second continuous generation) for mid-2026.
- **NVIDIA's Alpamayo advances open AV perception**: The open reasoning-model family for autonomous vehicles, paired with 100TB of open multimodal sensor data, continues gaining adoption in driving-perception research.

## Detailed Updates

### The 2026 video-generation race tightens
Four models now define the field: Seedance 2.0 (ByteDance), Sora 2 (OpenAI), Kling 3.0 (Kuaishou), and Veo 3.1 (Google). Seedance 2.0 holds the #1 spot on the with-audio leaderboard, while Alibaba's HappyHorse-1.0 leads without audio. Production workflows are stratifying into storyboard, generation, and orchestration layers, and video-to-video models are becoming the practical workhorse for creators. (WaveSpeed, Pinggy, TBS News)

### Kling 3.0 and the road to Wan 3.0
Kling 3.0 ships native 4K at 60fps with 15-second clips and multilingual lip-sync. Alibaba's Wan 3.0, targeted for mid-2026, reportedly brings 60B parameters, native 4K, and 30-second continuous generation — which would make it the longest-form open generation model available. (Pinggy)

### NVIDIA Alpamayo and open AV data
NVIDIA's Alpamayo open reasoning-model family for autonomous vehicle development — backed by one of the largest open multimodal datasets including 100 terabytes of vehicle sensor data — is anchoring a wave of open AV perception research following its CES 2026 debut. (NVIDIA Blog, InfoQ)

### Vision-language supervision improves 3D detection
New research in Science China Information Sciences proposes a vision-language supervision (VLS) paradigm for multimodal 3D object detection: multi-sensory proposals are refined with text-referred expressions and contrastive multimodal correspondences, improving robustness for driving perception. (Springer)

### VLA models consolidate in driving research
A new survey maps the rise of Vision-Language-Action frameworks for autonomous driving, which fuse perception with language-grounded decision-making for more interpretable driving policies — alongside work on multimodal sensor fusion with digital-twin simulation and explainable AI for AV perception. (arXiv, Nature Scientific Reports)
