# Computer Vision Weekly Update — July 5, 2026

## Highlights

- **ByteDance opens Seedance 2.5 to the public**: The headline vision story of the week. Seedance 2.5 entered its public launch window on July 3 via Dreamina and Jimeng, generating native 4K, unbroken 30-second clips in a single pass and accepting up to 50 multimodal reference inputs. It resets expectations for long-form, single-shot AI video.
- **Google refreshes its image-generation lineup while Gemini 3.5 Pro slips**: Google shipped image-model updates at the end of June — including publishing the Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite Image model card on June 30 — while the anticipated Gemini 3.5 Pro was pushed to later in July after enterprise testers flagged excessive token consumption in agentic workloads.
- **Gaussian splatting becomes standard tooling**: The June wrap-up published this week shows radiance-field tech crossing into mainstream pipelines: Houdini 22 made splats a headline feature, Arnold added native splat rendering, and ComfyUI gained native splat generation — alongside converging file-format standardization efforts.

## Detailed Updates

### Seedance 2.5 launches: native 30-second 4K video, no stitching
ByteDance's Seedance 2.5, unveiled at the Volcano Engine FORCE conference in late June, hit its public launch window on July 3. The model generates a continuous 30-second clip at native 4K with no segment stitching or visible seams, and accepts up to 50 reference inputs — images, audio clips, 3D white models, and style references — up from 12 in its predecessor. Audio is co-processed in the same latent space as visuals, yielding natively synchronized sound effects. Coverage from Tech Times notes the launch arrives under a copyright cloud, as rights holders scrutinize training data and output likeness. ByteDance skipped four version numbers to signal a generational leap, and early comparisons position it directly against Google's Veo 3.1 and Kling 3.5.

### Google's image-model shuffle and the Gemini 3.5 Pro delay
Per this week's daily AI roundups, Google rolled out updated image-generation models on June 30, and DeepMind published the Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite Image model card the same day — a high-volume, lower-cost tier of its "Nano Banana" image family. Meanwhile, Gemini 3.5 Pro missed its promised June launch and is now expected later in July, after enterprise testers reported runaway token consumption in extended agentic tasks. The delay matters for multimodal roadmaps across Google AI Studio and Vertex AI.

### Gaussian splatting goes mainstream in production 3D tools
The Radiance Fields monthly recap published this week documents a step change in 3D reconstruction tooling. SideFX made Gaussian splats a headline strategic initiative in Houdini 22, rebuilding a limited render experiment into a full toolset spanning rigging, animation, simulation, relighting, and compositing — including reconstructing splats from Karma CG renders with embedded normals and world-space attributes. Autodesk's Arnold 7.5.2 added native splat rendering and relighting, and ComfyUI shipped native splat generation via TripoSplat plus manipulation nodes. Three parallel standardization efforts are converging to make splats a first-class citizen in 3D data pipelines.

### FDA clears MRI-based AI for brain metastases as radiology extends its lead
The FDA granted 510(k) clearance to Brain Mets, AI software that detects, measures, and contours brain metastases on routine MRI scans — relevant to up to 40 percent of adult cancer patients, per Diagnostic Imaging. It lands amid a torrid pace of imaging-AI approvals: radiology accounts for roughly 1,163 of the 1,524 FDA-cleared AI algorithms tracked this year, with 68 new radiology clearances in Q1 alone, according to Radiology Business.

### Multimodal model race heats up: Happy Horse and Muse Spark chatter
July's model-release tracking highlights growing chatter around Alibaba's Happy Horse 1.0 and Meta's Muse Spark, both pointing toward stronger image, video, and mixed-media capability. The competitive framing this week: Google pushing speed and product embedding, ByteDance pushing long-form video, and challengers racing to integrated multimodal workflows rather than single-task models.

### Autonomous driving vision rides the VLA wave
Industry coverage this week continued to frame 2026 as a turning point for camera-driven autonomy. Design News reports the global robotaxi fleet is expected to grow tenfold by 2030, with Waymo targeting one million weekly rides across 27 US cities by year-end. Wayve's CEO says vision-based driving models are improving "by orders of magnitude every year," while NVIDIA's open Alpamayo reasoning VLA models — debuting in the Mercedes-Benz CLA — anchor the shift toward explainable, reasoning-based perception stacks.

### Spatial computing pivots from demos to inspection and training
A July industry roundup finds spatial-computing investment moving away from flashy headset demos toward vision systems that help workers train faster, inspect better, and review 3D work in manufacturing, construction, and healthcare — a pragmatic turn for applied computer vision in the enterprise.
