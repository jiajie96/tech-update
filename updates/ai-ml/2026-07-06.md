# AI & Machine Learning Weekly Update — July 6, 2026

## Highlights

- **Anthropic launches Claude Sonnet 5**: Released June 30/July 1, Sonnet 5 is Anthropic's most agentic model yet, autonomously using tools like browsers and terminals while delivering near-Opus 4.8 performance at significantly lower cost. Separately, the US government lifted national security restrictions on Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models on July 1 after nearly three weeks offline.
- **OpenAI unveils "Jalapeño" inference chip**: Announced July 1, OpenAI's custom silicon marks a major step toward reducing dependence on external chip suppliers as the company gears up for its IPO.
- **China's GLM-5.2 fuels catch-up debate**: Z.ai's inexpensive GLM-5.2 demonstrates capabilities competitive with leading US frontier models, intensifying debate over whether China is closing the AI gap.

## Detailed Updates

### Claude Sonnet 5 arrives; Fable 5 restrictions lifted
Anthropic launched Claude Sonnet 5 around July 1, positioning it as the company's most agentic model to date — capable of autonomously operating browsers and terminals — with near-Opus 4.8 performance at much lower cost. In parallel, the US Commerce Department lifted export controls on Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 on July 1, restoring availability after roughly three weeks. (Sources: llm-stats.com, aiweekly.co)

### OpenAI announces Jalapeño inference chip
On July 1, OpenAI announced its custom Jalapeño inference chip, joining the ranks of AI labs designing in-house silicon to control inference costs and supply. The move comes as OpenAI brings on senior hires ahead of a widely anticipated IPO, and as Fortune reports Sam Altman is seeking a "new world order for AI" while OpenAI slowly loses ground to Google and Anthropic. (Fortune, TechCrunch)

### White House pushes voluntary frontier-model standards
The White House is in advanced talks with OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google on voluntary frontier-model standards — a framework covering benchmarks, release timelines, and domestic/foreign access rules — with an announcement expected as soon as next week. (dentro.de/ai)

### Meta's "Watermelon" reportedly matches GPT-5.5
Business Insider reported July 2 that Meta Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang told a closed briefing that Meta's frontier model in training, codenamed Watermelon, matches GPT-5.5 on current evaluations and uses an order of magnitude more compute than Meta's previous frontier model. (buildfastwithai.com)

### China's GLM-5.2 and Meituan's LongCat-2.0 shake up rankings
Z.ai's GLM-5.2 has become the centerpiece of the US-China AI race debate, offering frontier-competitive capability at low cost. Meanwhile, Meituan revealed that its LongCat-2.0 was the anonymous "Owl Alpha" model that topped OpenRouter developer usage rankings for weeks before its identity was disclosed. (llm-stats.com)

### Anthropic launches Claude Science
Anthropic launched Claude Science, a dedicated application for scientific research workflows focused on drug discovery, protein structure analysis, genomics, and computational biology — part of CEO Dario Amodei's goal of compressing life-sciences R&D cycles by 10x. Launch partners include Helix's GenoSphere connector, giving approved users access to 500,000+ linked clinico-genomic records. (crescendo.ai)

### AI finds a 29-year-old vulnerability
Squidbleed (CVE-2026-47729), a 29-year-old memory leak in the Squid proxy server, was discovered by Claude Mythos 5 under Anthropic's Project Glasswing defensive security research program and disclosed in late June — a notable demonstration of frontier models doing real vulnerability research. (buildfastwithai.com)

### Personnel: Dean Ball joins OpenAI
Policy researcher Dean Ball announced July 6 he is joining OpenAI to lead a new Strategic Futures team reporting to Chief Strategy Officer Jason Kwon, focusing on catastrophic risk, recursive self-improvement, labor market impact, and lab-government relations. (dentro.de/ai)
