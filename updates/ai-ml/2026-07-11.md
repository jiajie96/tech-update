# AI & Machine Learning Weekly Update — July 11, 2026

## Highlights

- **OpenAI ships GPT-5.6 family to general availability**: The GPT-5.6 lineup (Sol, Terra, Luna) went GA on July 9 and is now ChatGPT's default model, with an agent-focused ChatGPT Work product powered by Codex following close behind. This resets the frontier baseline for the second half of 2026.
- **Meta launches Muse Spark 1.1 as its first paid model**: Shipped July 9 at $1.25/$4.25 per million tokens, Muse Spark 1.1 is a multimodal reasoning model with a self-managed 1M-token context window and native agent/subagent orchestration with MCP support — Meta's clearest move yet into monetized frontier models.
- **Google delays Gemini 3.5 Pro to July 17 for a ground-up rebuild**: DeepMind abandoned the Gemini 2.5 Pro architecture in favor of a full architectural redesign, pushing the launch to July 17.

## Detailed Updates

### GPT-5.6 goes GA and becomes ChatGPT's default
OpenAI opened its GPT-5.6 family — Sol, Terra, and Luna — to general availability on July 9, making it the default across ChatGPT. Alongside the release, OpenAI is launching ChatGPT Work, an agent-based enterprise product powered by Codex and GPT-5.6. The staggered naming suggests a tiered capability/latency split similar to prior families. (via llm-stats and ThursdAI release trackers)

### Meta's Muse Spark 1.1 arrives as a paid multimodal reasoner
Meta shipped Muse Spark 1.1 on July 9 as its first paid model, priced at $1.25 per 1M input and $4.25 per 1M output tokens. The model features a self-managed 1M-token context window, native primary-agent and subagent orchestration, and MCP plus custom-skill support — positioning Meta Superintelligence Labs squarely against OpenAI and Anthropic in the agentic model market.

### Gemini 3.5 Pro pushed to July 17 after architectural rebuild
Google DeepMind delayed its next-generation Gemini 3.5 Pro to July 17, 2026, after deciding to abandon the existing Gemini 2.5 Pro architecture for a ground-up redesign. The delay underscores how quickly architecture churn is accelerating at the frontier. (BigGo Finance)

### Anthropic's Sonnet 5 lands with aggressive pricing; Mythos flagship back online
Anthropic launched Sonnet 5 with near-Opus 4.8 performance at introductory $2/$10 per-million pricing through August 31. Separately, on July 1 Anthropic redeployed its Mythos-class flagship after the US government lifted the June 12 export-control order that had pulled the model offline for nearly three weeks — a notable episode in AI policy intersecting with commercial deployment.

### China's GLM-5.2 fuels the catch-up debate
Z.ai's GLM-5.2 has become the centerpiece of a growing debate over whether China is closing the frontier gap with US labs, with strong benchmark showings driving the discussion.

### Chip and infrastructure money keeps flowing
SambaNova raised $1B at an $11B valuation for inference chips, Nvidia-backed Nscale locked in $900M for data-center buildout, and SK hynix raised $26.5B in a US listing to expand high-bandwidth memory production. Microsoft expects roughly $190B in capex for calendar 2026, up 61% year over year, even as investors question whether enterprise monetization can keep pace. (Crunchbase, Fortune, SiliconANGLE)

### Research: interpretability and long-context efficiency
Notable recent research includes automated circuit-discovery algorithms that identify interpretable computational subgraphs in models up to 100B parameters, and an architecture allowing a 70B model to process 1M tokens at roughly the compute cost of a 32K window — flagged as one of the most practically impactful LLM papers of 2026 so far. A constitutional-distillation framework that teaches value-aligned behavior from a written constitution without armies of human raters also drew attention. (Sebastian Raschka's 2026 papers list, Engineer Master Labs)
