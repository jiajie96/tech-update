# Gaming Weekly Update — July 4, 2026

## Highlights

- **Sony to end physical PlayStation disc production in 2028**: Announced June 30, the move marks the definitive end of the disc era for PlayStation and accelerates the industry's all-digital shift.
- **Xbox pulls out of IO Interactive's 'Project Fantasy'**: Microsoft withdrew from publishing the Hitman studio's ambitious online fantasy game on June 30, the latest sign of tightened first-party spending.
- **Palworld hits 1.0 on July 10**: Pocketpair's monster-collector finally leaves early access with 27 pages of patch notes, new weapons and gear, and a Wing Pack for aerial traversal.

## Detailed Updates

### Sony winds down physical media
Sony announced June 30 it will end production of physical PlayStation discs in 2028. Combined with retail partners already shrinking shelf space, the decision effectively sets an expiration date on console physical media and has major implications for game preservation and secondhand markets. (Bloomberg Gaming)

### Xbox cancels Project Fantasy publishing deal
Microsoft pulled out of IO Interactive's 'Project Fantasy' on June 30. For IO — which staffed up substantially around the project alongside its 007 game — the withdrawal is a significant blow and continues a pattern of platform holders cutting external commitments. (Bloomberg Gaming)

### July release slate: Palworld 1.0, Halo remake, AC remake
Palworld's 1.0 launch lands July 10 with sweeping additions. Halo: Campaign Evolved, a full remake of the 2001 original with new extra missions, arrives July 28. Ubisoft's Assassin's Creed remake also ships this month, headlining an otherwise co-op-horror-heavy July. (GameSpot, PC Gamer)

### Consolidation watch: Wemade and GameStop-eBay
A NeoPulse-led group agreed June 26 to acquire a stake in Korean publisher Wemade. Meanwhile GameStop's proposed acquisition of eBay remains in focus, with CEO Ryan Cohen concentrating leadership on operating performance and the deal. (Bloomberg Gaming, SEC filings)

### GDC 2026 data: engines and AI sentiment
The GDC State of the Game Industry report (2,300+ professionals) shows Unreal Engine as primary engine for 42% of developers versus Unity's 30%, with Unreal dominant at AA/AAA studios. Over one in four respondents were laid off in the past two years. On AI: 51% plan to use AI for level design within two years, yet 52% of developers now view generative AI negatively — adoption and unease rising together. (GDC, Sean Kim)

### AI tooling reshapes world-building economics
Houdini's AI tools are delivering level-generation speeds around 5x traditional methods, and automated terrain systems can produce 100km-wide maps in under an hour — compressing months of environment art and putting AAA-scale worlds within reach of AA budgets. (Whimsy Games, GameSpace)
